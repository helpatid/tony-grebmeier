Consistency creates safety.
All you have is your word.
So be your word.

My Nana. People knew her as Peggy. She was phenomenal.

She lived in an apartment in Santa Cruz, right between my dad’s place and my mom’s house. Some days my dad dropped me at school. Some days my mom did. Nana picked me up. We’d walk back to her apartment, play games, cook dinner, and then my mom would come get me.

Later in life people asked me which relationship I missed the most. Always my Nana. She cared, and she showed me what hard work looked like. She was a real estate agent, and she was one of the only people I knew who was never a second late. If she was going to be late, she called ahead.

I never had to wonder where my ride was. Somebody would say, “Your Nana called, she’s running late, let’s play another game.” That’s what it feels like when consistency gets modeled for you. Even today, if I’m fifteen minutes out from a dinner reservation, I pick up the phone.

Ethan and Jenny, these stories are for you. There was a time I wasn’t consistent. I ran late a lot. I was out of integrity. Now if I’m running behind, people know. Then I work on not running behind at all.

You can be the model someone else looks up to. All you have is your word. So be your word.

Is there a place in your life right now where you’re not consistent? You don’t have to wait for some big thing. Write down what you’re late on. Work on it one day at a time. See what begins to unfold.
